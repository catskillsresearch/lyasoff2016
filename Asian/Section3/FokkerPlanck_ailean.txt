**Theorem** (`ae_integrableOn_exp_bm`). for σ > 0 and ν < 0, almost every path s ↦ e^{σ W_s + ν s} is Lebesgue integrable on (0, ∞), because W_s / s → 0 a.s.

**Theorem** (`infinite_integral_finite`). for σ > 0 and ν < 0 the improper integral ∫₀^∞ e^{σW+νs} is a.s. finite.

**Theorem** (`isBrownianReal_map_pathFunctional_eq`). Any two Brownian motions induce the same law for a measurable functional of the continuous path. `F` and `G` are random variables that coincide (a.s.) with `Φ` evaluated at any continuous path agreeing with `W`, resp. `B`.

**Theorem** (`indepFun_path_of_ae_eq`). Independence of two processes (in the product σ-algebra of `ℝ≥0 → ℝ`) transfers to any `C(ℝ≥0, ℝ)`-valued versions of them.

**Theorem** (`integral_Ioi_exp_split`). Deterministic Markov-type decomposition of the perpetuity at time `t`: `∫₀^∞ e^{σ w_s + ν s} ds = ∫₀ᵗ e^{σ w_s + ν s} ds + e^{σ w_t + ν t} ∫₀^∞ e^{σ (w_{t+s} − w_t) + ν s} ds`.

**Theorem** (`Z_stationary_consistent`). with the *same* drift ν in the perpetuity and in the perturbation, i.e. Z_t = e^{νt − σB_t} (∫₀^∞ e^{σW_s+νs} ds + ∫₀ᵗ e^{−νs+σB_s} ds), Z_t has the law of ∫₀^∞ e^{σW_s+νs} ds for every t. Proof: `β_u = B_{t−u} − B_t` (negated time reversal of B) is a BM independent of W and Z_t = ∫₀ᵗ e^{σβ_u+νu} du + e^{σβ_t+νt} A_∞(W); by the weak Markov property the perpetuity of a single BM has the same decomposition with the same joint law of the ingredients.

**Theorem** (`Z_stationary`). Z_t has the same law as ∫₀^∞ e^{σW+νs} ds.

**Theorem** (`ae_perpetuity_pos`). The Dufresne perpetuity is strictly positive a.s.: continuous paths make the integrand bounded below by a positive constant on `[0,1]`, so the improper integral is positive.

**Theorem** (`aemeasurable_perpetuity`). The map `ω ↦ ∫₀^∞ e^{W_s+νs} ds` is a.e. equal to `perpetuityPath ν ∘ bmPath`.

**Theorem** (`pathwise_log_Z_identity`). Deterministic pathwise identity: writing `Y_t = A + ∫₀ᵗ e^{-νs+B_s}` and `Z_t = e^{νt−B_t} Y_t`, one has `log Z_t − log A = νt − B_t + ∫₀ᵗ Z_s⁻¹` by ordinary calculus.

**Theorem** (`ae_perpetuity_ge_expInt`). Finite-horizon lower bound: `A ≥ e^ν ∫₀¹ e^{W_s} ds` a.s. when `ν < 0`.

**Theorem** (`integrable_inv_perpetuity`). Integrability of the reciprocal perpetuity: bound by the finite-horizon negative moment.

**Theorem** (`integrable_BM_eval`). Brownian evaluation is integrable (Gaussian moments).

**Theorem** (`integral_BM_eval`). Mean-zero property of Brownian motion.

**Theorem** (`aemeasurable_Zproc`). `Zproc` is a.e. measurable (path-integral construction).

**Theorem** (`integral_inv_Zproc_eq`). Reciprocal moments of `Z_t` and `A` agree by stationarity.

**Theorem** (`integrable_inv_Zproc`). Slice integrability of `Z_t⁻¹` (same L¹ norm as `A⁻¹` by stationarity).

**Theorem** (`integrable_uncurry_inv_Zproc`). Joint integrability of `(s, ω) ↦ Z_s(ω)⁻¹` on `(0, t] × Ω`, for Fubini.

**Theorem** (`perpetuity_inv_moment`). Stationarity of `Z` plus the pathwise log identity give `E[A⁻¹] = -ν`. Uses truncated logs + DCT so that only `Integrable A⁻¹` (not `Integrable log A`) is required.

**Abbreviation** (`dufresneGenerator`). Unit-volatility generator: `generator 1 ν`.

**Theorem** (`integrable_pow_inv_perpetuity`). Integrability of all negative integer moments of the perpetuity.

**Theorem** (`integral_pow_inv_Zproc_eq`). Reciprocal power moments of `Z_t` and `A` agree by stationarity.

**Theorem** (`pathwise_pow_inv_Y_identity`). Pathwise FTC: derivative of `Y ↦ Y⁻ᵏ` along the finite-variation factor of `Z`.

**Theorem** (`integral_wald_exp_eq_one`). Wald exponential for Brownian motion: `E[exp(α B_t − α² t / 2)] = 1`.

**Theorem** (`integrable_uncurry_pow_inv_Zproc`). Joint integrability of `(s, ω) ↦ Z_s(ω)⁻ᵏ` on `(0, t] × Ω`.

**Theorem** (`pathwise_Zpow_eq_wald_mul`). Algebraic identity: `Z_u^{-k} = M_u · G_u` with Wald factor `M` and FV factor `G`.

**Theorem** (`pathwise_Zpow_eq_wald_H`). Pathwise Wald–log factorization: `Z_t^{-k} = A^{-k} N_t H_t` with `N` the Wald exponential and `H_t = exp(c t - k ∫₀ᵗ Z^{-1})`.

**Theorem** (`pathwise_H_ftc`). Pathwise FTC for the finite-variation factor `H` in `pathwise_Zpow_eq_wald_H`.

**Theorem** (`integral_Ainv_mul_wald_eq`). On the product space, `A⁻ᵏ` (from the first BM) is independent of the Wald factor `N_t = exp(k B_t − k² t/2)` (from the second BM), and `E[A⁻ᵏ N_t] = E[A⁻ᵏ]`.

**Theorem** (`integral_waldIncrement_mul_Zpow_fixedA`). For fixed initial capital `A > 0`, the Wald increment after time `s` is independent of `Z_s` (which depends on `B` only through `[0, s]`), hence `E[D_{s,t} Z_s^{-m}] = E[Z_s^{-m}]`.

**Theorem** (`integrable_waldIncrement_mul_Zpow`). Integrability of the Wald-increment times `Z_s^{-m}` on the product space.

**Theorem** (`integral_waldIncrement_mul_Zpow`). Product-space lift: Wald increment after `s` is mean-one against `Z_s^{-m}`.

**Theorem** (`integrable_pow_inv_Zproc`). Integrability of reciprocal powers of the stationary process `Z`.

**Theorem** (`pathwise_Zpow_drift_identity`). Pathwise Dynkin identity: expand `Z_t^{-k} - A^{-k}` via Wald–H and the FTC for `H`.

**Theorem** (`integrable_uncurry_waldIncrement_mul_Zpow`). Joint integrability of Wald-increment × `Z_s^{-m}` on `(0,1] × Q`.

**Theorem** (`perpetuity_neg_moment_succ`). Generator recurrence for reciprocal moments of the unit-volatility perpetuity.

**Theorem** (`perpetuity_neg_moments`). Classical reciprocal-Gamma moments of the unit-volatility perpetuity: `E[A^{-n}] = gammaMoment (-2ν) 2 n` for `A = ∫₀^∞ e^{W_s+νs} ds`. Together with `measure_eq_of_moments` and `gammaMoment.lintegral_inv_pow_igMeasure` this identifies `law(A)` with `igMeasure (-2ν) 2` (= `dufresne 1 ν`).

**Theorem** (`dufresne_theorem_sigma_one`). The unit-volatility form of Dufresne's identity. This is the sole probabilistic identification needed after Brownian scaling: equivalently, the reciprocal of the integral has the Gamma law with shape `-2 * ν` and rate `2`. From `perpetuity_neg_moments`, compare the law of `A⁻¹` to the Gamma law via `measure_eq_of_moments`, then invert to conclude `P.map A = igMeasure (-2ν) 2`.

**Lemma** (`continuous_expectation_jointA_path`). Expectation of a continuous bounded functional of `(expInt, B_s)` is continuous in time.

**Theorem** (`joint_dynkin_Aop_product`). Dynkin identity (3.5) for product test functions. Proof outline (paper (3.5)): the product-test Itô identities (3.4a)/(3.4b) share the same LHS, so their drift-integrand difference integrates to zero on every horizon `τ ∈ (0,t]`. Pointwise that difference is `jointA` (`jointA.eq_twinGenerator_sub`). The resulting cumulative integral of `g(τ) := E[jointA(expInt_τ, W_τ)]` vanishes; continuity of `g` and FTC recover `g(t) = 0`.

**Lemma** (`integral_jointA_product_eq_integral_adjoint_of_contDiff`). Formal adjoint IBP for product tests against a globally `C²` density. Flux form: with `ϕ = u ⊗ v`, `a = σ²x²/2`, `b = σx`, `jointA ϕ · ψ − ϕ · adjoint ψ = ∂ₓ(a ∂ₓϕ ψ − ϕ · jointFlux ψ) + ∂ᵧ(b ∂ₓϕ ψ)`, and both divergence terms integrate to zero by compact support.

**Lemma** (`integral_jointA_product_eq_integral_adjoint`). Formal adjoint IBP for product tests with compact support in `jointDomain`. `∫ jointA(u⊗v) · φ = ∫ (u⊗v) · jointFokkerPlanckAdjoint(φ)`. `φ` is only smooth on the open half-plane, so it is first multiplied by an `x`-cutoff `χ` that equals `1` near `tsupport u` and vanishes near `x ≤ 0`; the cut-off density is globally `C²`, both integrands are unchanged, and the global flux-form IBP applies.
