Mathematical English for `ContourEq.lean` (Lean names in typewriter).

**Definition** (`contourEq`). ζ_a is the boundary of the strip {|ℑ z| < a}; since g_{t,p}(z̄) = −conj g_{t,p}(z) the upper edge contributes the conjugate of the lower one, leaving twice the real part. This is the only condition imposed on g_{t,p} (footnote 9). The integrand is also required to be integrable (pp. 1084–1085). PAPER NOTE: earlier versions of `contourEq` took a density `φc` and equated `laplaceCond φc y ξ` with `0`, resp. with `∫ ℜ((−4/ξ)^{p−1} · contourIntegrand)`. Neither is in the paper, and both are false for a genuine conditional density (at ξ = 0 they give `∫ φc(x|y) dx = 0`). (5.3) involves no density: it is a statement about g_{t,p}, the contour ζ_a and y only.

**Theorem** (`two`). residues of g_{t,2}(z) sinh z / (cosh z − cosh y) at z = ±y inside the strip; the vertical sides vanish (Gaussian decay) and the upper edge is the conjugate of the lower one. Proved in `ContourTwo.contour_two` for every opening 0 < a < 2π.

Proof idea: follow the paper argument encoded in the Lean proof of `two`.

**Theorem** (`three`). integrating d/du [g_{t,3}(z)/(cosh z − cosh y)] over the lower edge gives ∫ (integrand for p = 3) = −4 ∫ (integrand for p = 2) (`ContourRec.integral_three`).

Proof idea: follow the paper argument encoded in the Lean proof of `three`.

Proof idea: follow the paper argument encoded in the Lean proof of `four`.

**Theorem** (`g_solves_contour_equation`). g_{t,p} of (5.8) solves the contour equation (5.3) on ζ_a for every opening a ∈ [π/2, 3π/2] and every y ∈ ℝ. For p = 2 this is the residue computation at z = ±y (the vertical sides vanish and the upper edge is the conjugate of the lower one); for p = 3, 4 the relations g_{t,p}' = −4 sinh · g_{t,p−1} behind the recipe (5.7) reduce the edge integral to the one for p − 1 by integration by parts, with factor −4/(p−2).

Proof idea: follow the paper argument encoded in the Lean proof of `g_solves_contour_equation`.
